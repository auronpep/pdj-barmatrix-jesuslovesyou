Q14223: Commercial Speech Needs Fit

Outline: 44040502 / Key: D

A public library removes only commercial-ad postings from bulletin boards, citing clutter, but the policy clears only 10% of slots with no proof commercial flyers create more clutter. Is the policy constitutional?

Answer flow

  1. Classify the speech: commercial advertising for for-profit services.
  2. Use the commercial-speech framework, not rational basis or strict scrutiny.
  3. Accept that reducing clutter and paper waste can be a legitimate and substantial interest.
  4. Ask whether the policy directly advances that interest with a reasonable fit.
  5. Cut A because rational basis is too low.
  6. Cut B because strict scrutiny is too high.
  7. Cut C because least restrictive means is not the commercial-speech tailoring test.
  8. Choose D because the 15-of-150 clearance and missing proof show no reasonable fit.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Yes, because regulations of commercial speech are subject only to the requirement that they be rationally related to a legitimate state goal, and that requirement is satisfied here.
  • B / trap: Yes, because the library board has a compelling interest in protecting the appearance of its lobbies and reducing paper waste, and such a ban is necessary to vindicate this interest.
  • C / trap: No, because it does not constitute the least restrictive means with which to reduce clutter and paper waste in the library lobbies.
  • D / correct: No, because there is not a reasonable fit between the legitimate interest of the library board in reducing clutter and paper waste and the means it chose to advance that interest.

Q14224: Content Neutral Still Needs Fit

Outline: 44040502 / Key: B

A city applies a color and size limit to all signs in a historic district. What is the political speaker's strongest First Amendment argument?

Answer flow

  1. Classify the ordinance: it regulates sign color and size, not message.
  2. That points to a content-neutral time, place, and manner lane.
  3. Do not use strict scrutiny unless the regulation is content based or otherwise triggers that tier.
  4. Cut A because least restrictive means and compelling interest are too demanding here.
  5. Cut C because there is no permit or licensing scheme.
  6. Cut D because the ordinance does not classify speech by subject or viewpoint.
  7. Ask whether the rule is narrowly tailored and leaves open alternatives.
  8. Choose B.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: The ordinance is not the least restrictive means of promoting a compelling government interest.
  • B / correct: The ordinance is not narrowly tailored to further an important government interest, nor does it leave open alternative channels of communication.
  • C / trap: The ordinance imposes a prior restraint on political expression.
  • D / trap: The ordinance effectively favors some categories of speech over others.

Q14225: Fair Hearing Means Testing The Witnesses

Outline: 44040203 / Key: C

A state agency revokes a professional license based only on statements from unnamed absent informants. Does the licensee's inability to question them violate procedural due process?

Answer flow

  1. Start with what the state took: Timothy's professional tutor license.
  2. Treat the license as a protected property interest.
  3. Ask what process the Commission gave before revocation.
  4. The only evidence was statements from unnamed absent informants.
  5. Timothy had no meaningful way to test credibility through questioning.
  6. Cut full faith and credit because a prosecutor's declination is not the kind of judgment the Commission must honor.
  7. Cut Article II and preemption because they do not address the hearing defect.
  8. Choose C.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: The administrative license revocation proceeding was invalid because it denied full faith and credit to the dismissal of the criminal charges by the United States Attorney.
  • B / trap: Article II requires a penalty of this kind to be imposed by a court rather than by an administrative agency.
  • C / correct: Timothy's inability to cross-examine his accusers denied him a fair hearing and caused the deprivation of his tutor license without due process.
  • D / trap: Federal laws penalizing the illegal sale of methamphetamine preempt state action relating to drug trafficking of the kind involved.

Q14231: State Action Before Scrutiny

Outline: 44040100 / Key: B

Does equal protection apply to a private insurer's risk-based pricing decision when the state only audits the insurer for solvency?

Answer flow

  1. Start with the actor: Martha sued Covenant Mutual.
  2. Covenant Mutual is a private insurer.
  3. The state audits solvency but does not set premiums or policy terms.
  4. That means the challenged premium is not the state's act.
  5. Resolve constitutional reach before rational-basis scrutiny.
  6. Cut the sheriff detour and private-discrimination overclaim.
  7. Choose B.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: No, because the different theft rates in the two areas provide a rational basis for the different premiums.
  • B / correct: No, because the Equal Protection Clause does not apply to this private insurer's pricing decision.
  • C / trap: Yes, because the higher theft rate in Martha's neighborhood shows that the county sheriff is not giving residents there the equal protection of the laws.
  • D / trap: Yes, because charging a higher premium based on a customer's residential area is inherently discriminatory.

Q14233: Public Money Does Not Decide the Act

Outline: 44040100 / Key: A

Does a one-time state grant to a privately owned team make the team's later private contractor-selection decisions state action?

Answer flow

  1. Start with the sued-over act: contractor selection.
  2. Identify who made that act: the private owners.
  3. Separate the public grant from the later private contracting decision.
  4. Ask whether the state controlled, encouraged, or participated in that decision.
  5. No fact supplies that missing link.
  6. Cut popularity, political-question, and grant-nexus overreads.
  7. Choose A.

Choice decode

  • A / correct: In the absence of additional evidence that the state controlled, encouraged, or participated in the team owners' contracting decisions, the one-time grant is insufficient to warrant treating those decisions as subject to the limitations of the Fourteenth Amendment.
  • B / trap: The intense public preoccupation with professional e-sports teams, coupled with the fact that live gaming finals have become a favorite modern pastime, is sufficient to justify application of the Fourteenth Amendment to the activities of professional e-sports teams.
  • C / trap: The issues presented by this case are nonjusticiable political questions because the fieldhouse grant followed a partisan legislative fight and there is a lack of judicially manageable standards to resolve the dispute.
  • D / trap: The nexus between the contracting decisions of the team owners and the one-time grant of state funds is sufficiently substantial to subject those decisions to the limitations of the Fourteenth Amendment.

Q14234: No Trigger Means Rational Basis

Outline: 44040301 / Key: B

A city denied a fully compliant permit for a reentry residence. What Equal Protection burden applies when no suspect or quasi-suspect class and no fundamental right are involved?

Answer flow

  1. Read the call as a burden-of-persuasion question.
  2. Sort the answers by burden: A, C, and D put heightened scrutiny on the government; B puts rational-basis burden on Daniel.
  3. Ask what fact elevates the Equal Protection scrutiny tier.
  4. Housing is not a recognized fundamental right for this burden question.
  5. Reentry or parole-transition status is not a suspect or quasi-suspect class.
  6. No heightened trigger remains.
  7. Use rational basis.
  8. Choose B because Daniel must prove no rational relation to a legitimate governmental interest.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Because the commission's action effectively discriminates against a quasi-suspect class in relation to a basic subsistence need, the commission must prove that the denial is substantially related to an important governmental interest.
  • B / correct: Because the commission's action is in the nature of economic or social welfare regulation, Daniel must prove that the denial is not rationally related to a legitimate governmental interest.
  • C / trap: Because housing is a fundamental right, the commission must prove that the denial is necessary to serve a compelling governmental interest.
  • D / trap: Because the commission's action invidiously discriminates against a suspect class, the commission must prove that the denial is necessary to serve a compelling governmental interest.

Q14236: Free Exercise Needs Targeting

Outline: 44040501 / Key: C

A federal land-use permit will eliminate a sincere worship site on public land. What must the community show to win a First Amendment Free Exercise claim?

Answer flow

  1. Read the claim: First Amendment Free Exercise only.
  2. Confirm the government concedes sincerity and severe adverse effect.
  3. Do not stop there; severe burden alone is not enough under the First Amendment rule for neutral, generally applicable action.
  4. Cut A because the case is not solved by burden-versus-interest balancing.
  5. Cut B because impact is not intent.
  6. Cut D because least-burdensome alternatives belong to strict scrutiny after the trigger.
  7. Ask what would trigger strict scrutiny: government action aimed at religion.
  8. Choose C.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: The burden on the community's religious exercise from the logging operation outweighs the government's interest in permitting commercial timber harvesting.
  • B / trap: The logging operation will have a discriminatory impact on the community's religious practices in relation to the practices of other religious groups.
  • C / correct: The permit issued by the Forest Service is aimed at suppressing the community's religious practices.
  • D / trap: The government can serve its legitimate interest in timber harvesting by selecting a logging site that is less burdensome on the community's religious practices.

Q14237: Funding Is Not the Firing

Outline: 44040100 / Key: C

Does a private, state-accredited school that receives 25 percent of its budget from state funds become a state actor when it fires an instructor for a public statement, without state coercion or participation in the firing?

Answer flow

  1. Start with the claim Mary actually brings: a constitutional free-speech claim against a private institute.
  2. Circle the challenged act: the institute's decision to discharge Mary.
  3. Separate background government contacts from responsibility for that discharge.
  4. Accreditation and 25 percent operating support are not enough by themselves.
  5. Ask whether the state coerced, significantly encouraged, approved, or participated in the firing.
  6. No fact supplies that missing link.
  7. Cut the automatic-accreditation and private-autonomy reasons.
  8. Choose C.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: No, because the state's accreditation and partial funding of the institute are sufficient to make the state an active participant in Mary's discharge.
  • B / trap: No, because the U.S. Constitution provides a cause of action against any state-accredited school that restricts freedom of speech as a condition of employment.
  • C / correct: Yes, because the institute's discharge of Mary is not attributable to the state for purposes of the Fourteenth Amendment.
  • D / trap: Yes, because the First and Fourteenth Amendments affirmatively protect the institute's right to employ only instructors who share and communicate its views.

Q14242: Grandfather Lines Get Rational Basis

Outline: 44040305 / Key: A

Does Equal Protection invalidate an ordinary city vendor ordinance with a grandfather exemption when no suspect class or fundamental right is involved?

Answer flow

  1. Identify the government action: a city regulates mobile commercial stands on public walkways.
  2. Identify the classification: long-time vendors are grandfathered; newer vendors are excluded.
  3. Check for a suspect class.
  4. None appears.
  5. Check for a fundamental right.
  6. None is burdened.
  7. Route the case to rational basis, not strict scrutiny or substantial relationship.
  8. Choose A because the ordinance is plausibly tied to congestion control and local economic regulation.

Choice decode

  • A / correct: Constitutional, because its validity is governed by the rational basis test, and courts defer to economic choices in local legislation if they are even plausibly justifiable.
  • B / trap: Unconstitutional, because economic benefits or burdens imposed by legislatures through grandfather provisions have consistently been declared per se violations of the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.
  • C / trap: Constitutional, because it is narrowly tailored to implement the city's compelling interest in reducing pedestrian congestion and therefore satisfies the strict scrutiny test applicable to such cases.
  • D / trap: Unconstitutional, because the relationship between the legitimate purpose of the ordinance and the conduct it regulates is so tenuous and underinclusive that the ordinance fails the substantial relationship test applicable to such cases.

Q14275: Public Venue Is Not State Action

Outline: 44040100 / Key: B

A private group with a men-only leadership rule rents a city facility on neutral terms for a public officer induction; does that alone make the leadership rule unconstitutional state action?

Answer flow

  1. Start with the actor: the League is private.
  2. Circle the challenged act: the League's own officer-selection rule.
  3. Separate city ownership of the venue from responsibility for that rule.
  4. The city rents the dome to any group on first-come, hourly terms.
  5. No fact shows city coercion, encouragement, joint participation, public function, or entwinement.
  6. Cut public-invitation and association-overclaim answers.
  7. Choose B.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Yes, because once the League invited the public to the officer-installation ceremony, the Fourteenth Amendment barred it from excluding women from any leadership role connected to that event.
  • B / correct: No, because the League is a private organization, and its officer-selection rule is not state action merely because the ceremony uses a city facility rented on neutral terms.
  • C / trap: No, because freedom of association under the Fourteenth Amendment prohibits the city from placing any conditions on a private group's use of city facilities.
  • D / trap: Yes, because renting the city observatory for the officer-installation ceremony subjects the League's leadership rule to the Fourteenth Amendment.
