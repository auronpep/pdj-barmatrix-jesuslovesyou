Q14757: Transcript Layer Fails First

Outline: 33040103 / Key: B

A civil plaintiff offers a transcript from a former worker's criminal trial against the company, but the company was not a party to that trial and the worker was no longer employed when he testified. Is the transcript admissible?

Answer flow

  1. Start with the offered item: a criminal-trial transcript.
  2. Notice the transcript quotes a manager's instruction.
  3. Scan hearsay outside-in.
  4. Check Stephen's transcript testimony first.
  5. Cut the inner-statement-only answer.
  6. Cut the agent answer because Stephen testified after leaving the company.
  7. Cut former testimony because the company had no prior chance to develop the testimony.
  8. Choose B.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: admissible, because Lydia's instruction to Stephen is not hearsay.
  • B / correct: inadmissible, because it is hearsay not within any exception.
  • C / trap: admissible as a statement by Stephen as an agent of a party-opponent.
  • D / trap: admissible, although hearsay, as former testimony.

Q14763: Non-Hearsay Purpose: Notice Is Not Truth

Outline: 33040203 / Key: C

A business offers a copy of a notice letter and routine-practice testimony only to prove that notice was given. Is the copy admissible?

Answer flow

  1. Lock the offered purpose: the letter copy is offered on the issue of giving notice.
  2. Ask whether the letter is offered for the truth of its contents. It is not.
  3. Use the routine-practice fact to support the inference that the notice was sent.
  4. Cut D because a hearsay exception is unnecessary when the item is not hearsay.
  5. Cut B because the original-document rule is not the active frame for notice use.
  6. Choose C because notice purpose plus routine practice supplies the admissibility route.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: inadmissible, because it is hearsay not within any exception.
  • B / trap: inadmissible, because it is not the best evidence of the notice.
  • C / correct: admissible, because of the routine practices of the troupe.
  • D / trap: admissible, though hearsay, under the business record exception.

Q14765: Bias Impeachment: Leniency Promise

Outline: 31010503 / Key: B

When a prosecution witness has been promised dismissal of her own pending charge after she testifies, may the defendant use that promise to impeach her for bias?

Answer flow

  1. Identify Ruth's status: she is a prosecution witness.
  2. Identify the benefit: her same-arson charge may be dismissed after Daniel's trial.
  3. Ask why Daniel offers the promise: to show Ruth's motive to favor the prosecution.
  4. Classify the use as bias impeachment, not truth of the dismissal promise.
  5. Cut A because hearsay depends on purpose of offer.
  6. Cut C because settlement or plea policy does not erase witness-bias impeachment.
  7. Cut D because party-opponent admission is the wrong admissibility theory.
  8. Choose B.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Inadmissible, because the promise is hearsay not within any exception.
  • B / correct: Admissible, as proper impeachment of Ruth.
  • C / trap: Inadmissible, because the law encourages negotiated resolutions of criminal charges.
  • D / trap: Admissible, as a statement by an agent of a party-opponent.

Q14767: Own-Fault Statement Against Interest

Outline: 33040301 / Key: C

When an unavailable injury victim admits he caused the accident in a way that would defeat his own claim, which hearsay exception admits the statement?

Answer flow

  1. Start with unavailability: Paul died.
  2. Read the statement for what it proves.
  3. Notice Paul admitted his own fault.
  4. Tie that admission to civil liability or defeat of the claim.
  5. Cut dying declaration because there is no belief of imminent death.
  6. Cut excited utterance because the timing and stress facts are missing.
  7. Cut state of mind because the statement proves a past fact.
  8. Choose C.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: a statement under belief of impending death.
  • B / trap: an excited utterance.
  • C / correct: a statement against interest.
  • D / trap: a statement of Paul's then-existing state of mind.

Q14769: One Recording, Two Exceptions

Outline: 33040302 / Key: D

A witness accurately recorded an identifying number while perceiving an event, later cannot remember the number, and the proponent offers the recording at trial. Is the recording admissible under one exception, both, or neither?

Answer flow

  1. Start with the offered audio recording.
  2. Ask whether it describes the event while or immediately after Stephen perceived it.
  3. Mark present sense impression: yes.
  4. Ask whether Stephen once knew the tag number.
  5. Use his current memory failure and accurate fresh recording.
  6. Mark recorded recollection: yes.
  7. Cut the two only answers and the no-exception answer.
  8. Choose D.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Yes, as a recorded recollection only.
  • B / trap: No, because it is hearsay not within any exception.
  • C / trap: Yes, as a present sense impression only.
  • D / correct: Yes, as a present sense impression and as a recorded recollection.

Q14772: Non-Hearsay Purpose: Identity from a Name

Outline: 33040203 / Key: C

A missing person told his heir a pseudonym he intended to use. That pseudonym later appeared on a passenger log for a vessel that disappeared. Is testimony about the conversation admissible?

Answer flow

  1. Identify the fact Esther needs to prove: Daniel's connection to the trawler name.
  2. Ask whether Daniel's statement is offered to prove its content. It is not.
  3. Use the statement as circumstantial evidence linking Daniel to the passenger log.
  4. Cut B because the auto-hearsay label skips the purpose-of-offer test.
  5. Cut A because Daniel is not the opposing party and the admission route is unnecessary.
  6. Choose C because the statement is non-hearsay identity evidence.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Inadmissible as a party admission, because Daniel's out-of-court statement to Esther binds him as a party.
  • B / trap: Inadmissible, because it is hearsay not within any exception.
  • C / correct: Admissible as circumstantial evidence that Daniel was on the trawler.
  • D / trap: Inadmissible, because Daniel has not been missing for more than seven years.

Q14777: Double Hearsay, One Layer At A Time

Outline: 33040301 / Key: C

Two conspirators agree to sell stolen property. One flees. The prosecutor offers the fugitive's spouse testimony that the fugitive repeated the defendant's money-owed statement. Is the testimony admissible?

Answer flow

  1. Start with the witness's testimony.
  2. Split the double hearsay into two layers.
  3. Layer one: Peter's statement to Daniel.
  4. Use party-opponent treatment for Peter's own statement offered against Peter.
  5. Layer two: Daniel's statement to Ruth.
  6. Reject co-conspirator treatment because the statement was not in furtherance.
  7. Use statement against interest because Daniel is unavailable and the statement hurts him.
  8. Choose C.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: inadmissible, because Daniel's statement was not in furtherance of the conspiracy.
  • B / trap: inadmissible, because Daniel is not shown to have firsthand knowledge that the manuscript was stolen.
  • C / correct: admissible, as evidence of a statement against interest by Daniel.
  • D / trap: admissible, as evidence of a statement by a party-opponent.

Q14778: Non-Hearsay Purpose: Reason for Refusal

Outline: 33040203 / Key: C

If a hiring defendant offers a former supervisor's warning only to explain why he refused to hire the plaintiff, is the warning hearsay or admissible for the hiring reason?

Answer flow

  1. Identify the consequential fact: Peter's asserted reason for refusing to hire Naomi.
  2. Ask why Ruth's warning is offered. It is offered to show Peter's reason, not to prove Naomi's actual ability.
  3. Classify the warning as a non-hearsay listener-reason use.
  4. Cut B because the hearsay-exception reflex skips the truth-purpose threshold.
  5. Cut A because it converts the warning into proof of the asserted work-capacity fact.
  6. Cut D because Peter can testify to what he heard and why he acted.
  7. Choose C because the warning explains Peter's hiring decision.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Admissible as evidence that Naomi could not keep up with lifting packed hymnals for an entire predawn route.
  • B / trap: Inadmissible, because Ruth's warning is hearsay not within any exception.
  • C / correct: Admissible as evidence of Peter's reason for refusing to hire Naomi.
  • D / trap: Inadmissible, because Peter's view of Naomi's abilities is not based on personal knowledge.

Q14781: Confession With A Witness

Outline: 33040301 / Key: C

An unavailable declarant told his spouse, in the presence of a third party, that the defendant paid him to commit arson. Is the spouse's testimony admissible, and under which rule?

Answer flow

  1. Start with the offered testimony from Ruth.
  2. Ask whether the spouse communication was confidential.
  3. Cut marital privilege because a chaplain was present.
  4. Ask whether the co-conspirator route applies.
  5. Cut co-conspirator treatment because the statement was a post-event confession.
  6. Check Daniel's unavailability.
  7. Use statement against interest because Daniel admitted arson for hire.
  8. Choose C.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: No, because the marital privilege survives the communicating spouse's death.
  • B / trap: No, because the statement was made after the conspiracy ended.
  • C / correct: Yes, because it is a statement against interest.
  • D / trap: Yes, because it is a statement by a coconspirator.

Q14791: Read Statements, Not the Handbook

Outline: 33040302 / Key: D

A party asks to let the jury examine an entire learned treatise after experts discuss its reliability. May the court send the publication itself to the jury?

Answer flow

  1. Start with the object Peter wants the jury to examine.
  2. Mark that the request is for the handbook itself.
  3. Separate a learned-treatise statement from the publication.
  4. Use the rule: statements may be read; the publication is not received as an exhibit.
  5. Reject the nonreliance no answer because it gives the wrong reason.
  6. Reject the two yes answers because reliability and jury weight do not override the exhibit limit.
  7. Choose D.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: No, because Peter's expert did not rely on the handbook in forming his opinion but instead relied on his own professional experience.
  • B / trap: Yes, because the jury decides the weight and credibility to give written and oral evidence.
  • C / trap: Yes, because an expert has testified that the handbook is a reliable authority.
  • D / correct: No, because the jury may consider only statements from the handbook that are read to it by counsel or by a witness.

Q14807: Non-Hearsay Purpose: Notice Needs a Limiting Instruction

Outline: 33040203 / Key: C

When prior complaint letters are offered to show notice rather than the truth of the complaints, should the court admit them for that limited purpose and give a timely requested limiting instruction?

Answer flow

  1. Identify the out-of-court statements: customer letters sent before this shipment.
  2. Ask why the letters are offered: to show the manufacturer had notice of similar incidents.
  3. Classify the use as non-hearsay because notice is different from proving the complaints were true.
  4. Overrule the hearsay objection for the limited notice purpose.
  5. Apply FRE 105 because the manufacturer made a timely limiting-instruction request.
  6. Cut A because it misses the non-hearsay notice purpose.
  7. Cut B because it omits the required limiting instruction.
  8. Cut D because the exhibit-reading limit is not the governing rule.
  9. Choose C.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Sustain the objection and treat the request for a limiting instruction as moot.
  • B / trap: Overrule the objection and deny the request for a limiting instruction.
  • C / correct: Overrule the objection and give the limiting instruction.
  • D / trap: Overrule the objection but allow only that the letters be read aloud, not received as exhibits.

Q14808: Non-Hearsay Purpose: Effect on the Listener

Outline: 33040203 / Key: C

A defendant offers three pieces of evidence to show what description the arresting officer heard, not whether the description was accurate. Which items are admissible?

Answer flow

  1. Lock the call phrase: on the issue of what description the deputy heard.
  2. Separate what the deputy heard from whether the robbery description was true.
  3. Classify each item as evidence of the communication and its effect on the listener.
  4. Keep the deputy's testimony because he can testify to what he heard.
  5. Keep the dispatcher's testimony because it shows what was broadcast.
  6. Keep the written note because it is not offered to prove the truth or terms of the writing.
  7. Choose C because every item is admissible for the limited non-hearsay purpose.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Only the deputy's testimony and the dispatcher's testimony.
  • B / trap: Only the dispatcher's testimony and the written note.
  • C / correct: All three items offered by the deputy.
  • D / trap: Only the deputy's testimony relating the description he heard.

Q14823: Witness Truthfulness: Bias vs. Specific Acts

Outline: 31010406 / Key: B

After a defendant's opinion character witness testifies that the defendant is honest, which rebuttal item is least likely to be admissible: bias evidence, a specific-act truthfulness attack, the defendant's contrary reputation, or the witness's reputation for untruthfulness?

Answer flow

  1. Lock the call: the question asks for the least likely admissible rebuttal item.
  2. Classify the attack before applying a rule: witness bias, witness truthfulness, defendant character rebuttal, or witness reputation.
  3. Cut A because the $7,500 testimony proves bias or motive, not character for truthfulness.
  4. Cut C because the defendant opened the character door with opinion testimony, allowing opposite-trait reputation rebuttal.
  5. Cut D because FRE 608(a) allows reputation or opinion proof about a witness's truthfulness.
  6. Choose B because it is extrinsic evidence of a specific act offered to attack Ruth's truthfulness, which FRE 608(b) bars.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: Testimony by Barnabas's former business partner that he overheard Ruth offer to provide favorable testimony if Barnabas would pay her $7,500.
  • B / correct: Testimony by Ruth's former co-worker that Ruth had submitted fabricated inventory counts eighteen months ago.
  • C / trap: Testimony by a sheriff's deputy that Barnabas has a long-standing reputation in the community for dishonesty.
  • D / trap: Testimony by a fellow church choir member that Ruth has a long-standing reputation in the community as an untruthful person.

Q14824: Preliminary Questions: Judge May Consider Hearsay

Outline: 31010107 / Key: B

A party offers a doctor's affidavit only to help the judge decide whether a dying statement is admissible. May the judge consider the hearsay affidavit?

Answer flow

  1. Lock the layer: the affidavit is offered to the court for an admissibility ruling.
  2. Apply FRE 104(a): the judge decides preliminary questions and is not bound by evidence rules except privilege.
  3. Cut C because the affidavit does not need a hearsay exception at this layer.
  4. Cut D because a right yes/no result with the wrong hearsay-exception reason is still wrong.
  5. Cut A because the federal dying-declaration exception is not limited to homicide prosecutions.
  6. Choose B because the judge may consider hearsay while ruling on the preliminary admissibility question.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: No, because statements made under belief of imminent death may be used only in prosecutions for homicide.
  • B / correct: Yes, because the judge may consider hearsay in ruling on preliminary questions.
  • C / trap: No, because the affidavit is hearsay and no hearsay exception covers it.
  • D / trap: Yes, because, although hearsay, the affidavit is a statement of then-existing mental condition.

Q14825: Refreshing Recollection: Before vs. During

Outline: 31010407 / Key: C

When a witness refreshes memory with a writing before testifying, when may or must the court let the adverse party examine the writing?

Answer flow

  1. Spot the timing fact: Lydia refreshed before she took the stand.
  2. Apply the Rule 612 timing split.
  3. Before testifying means the court may allow inspection if justice requires.
  4. While testifying would mean the adverse party is entitled to inspection.
  5. Cut A because authorship is not the trigger.
  6. Cut B because it imports recorded-recollection foundation.
  7. Cut D because it ignores the pre-testimony discretionary lane.
  8. Choose C.

Choice decode

  • A / trap: The court must allow the examination, but only to the extent that the report contains Lydia's own statement to Timothy.
  • B / trap: The court should not allow the examination, because the report was not shown to have been read and approved by Lydia while the matter was fresh in her mind.
  • C / correct: The court may allow the examination if the report was used by Lydia to refresh her memory before testifying, and must allow it if she used it during her testimony.
  • D / trap: The court should not allow the examination, unless the report was used by Lydia to refresh her memory while on the witness stand.
